    Connect WitFoo to ChatGPT and Claude¶

    Ask Claude or ChatGPT about your security environment and get answers built from your WitFoo appliance: open work units, what an attacker touched, your compliance posture, what your tools are catching. The AI app connects to WitFoo's MCP server (Model Context Protocol, the open standard these apps use to call outside tools). Every question runs as you, with your WitFoo permissions, and your security data stays on your appliance until a question asks for it.

    There are two ways to connect:

    Single connector address Your appliance's own address
    Address the app uses https://mcp-relay.witfoo.com/mcp, the same for every WitFoo customer Your appliance's address, for example https://mcp.example.com/mcp
    How you pick your appliance You type your appliance code at connect.witfoo.com The address is your appliance
    Needs analytics 1.2.1 and WFA 2.5.5 on the appliance, and the relay turned on analytics 1.2.0 or later, MCP turned on, and an address the AI app's servers can reach
    Passes through WitFoo's relay Yes No

    This page takes an administrator and a user through the single address:

    1. Check the prerequisites.
    2. Make sure Conductor ingests your data sources.
    3. Turn on the relay on the appliance (administrator).
    4. Allow app sign-in for your organization (administrator).
    5. Add WitFoo in Claude or ChatGPT and sign in (each user).

    To connect at your appliance's own address instead, see Connect at your appliance's own address.

    Requires analytics 1.2.1 or later

    The single connector address, https://mcp-relay.witfoo.com/mcp, with sign-in at connect.witfoo.com, works only with analytics 1.2.1 or later and WFA 2.5.5 or later on the appliance. An appliance on analytics 1.2.0 can connect apps only at its own address or at its per-appliance relay address (see Other ways to connect).

    How it works¶

    sequenceDiagram
    participant U as You (browser)
    participant A as Claude or ChatGPT
    participant R as WitFoo relay<br/>mcp-relay.witfoo.com
    participant C as connect.witfoo.com
    participant W as Your WitFoo appliance
    A->>R: Connect to https://mcp-relay.witfoo.com/mcp
    A->>U: Open the sign-in page
    U->>C: Type your appliance code
    C->>W: Read the appliance's sign-in address (through the relay)
    C->>U: "Continue to your appliance's own sign-in page"
    U->>W: Sign in to WitFoo and approve the app
    W->>A: Sign-in code (through your browser)
    A->>R: Exchange the code, then ask questions
    R->>W: Passed on to your appliance, answered there
    • One address for everyone. Every WitFoo customer adds the same address. You tell WitFoo which appliance is yours by typing your appliance code on the sign-in page.
    • No inbound port. Your appliance keeps one outbound connection open to WitFoo's relay. The AI app's requests travel down that connection, and the answers come back up it.
    • Your appliance is the only place you sign in. connect.witfoo.com never asks for your WitFoo username or password. It sends you to your appliance's own sign-in and approval page, which does not pass through WitFoo.
    • The relay keeps no sign-in request or token. It writes no sign-in request, token or network address to its database or logs. Its logs do record that each request to the sign-in page happened (path, time, status, sizes, duration and outcome, and your appliance's code once the code you typed names an appliance whose relay is on), and keep those lines for 30 days. See Privacy.

    Prerequisites¶

    What Requirement
    Licence Analytics Pro, Analytics Max or Reporter Pro. Other licences refuse MCP connections with feature_not_available. See MCP Licensing.
    Appliance software analytics 1.2.1 or later and WFA 2.5.5 or later.
    Network, appliance One outbound HTTPS connection to mcp-relay.witfoo.com on port 443. No inbound port. An HTTP proxy is not used. The appliance must also fetch each app's published client metadata document over HTTPS, directly (no forward proxy), so it needs outbound access to the app's metadata host.
    Network, users Each user's browser must reach the appliance's web UI, because the approval page is not relayed. Users off-site need whatever access (for example VPN) they normally use to reach WitFoo.
    Appliance sign-in page An https address for the appliance's web UI that users' browsers can reach (mcp_oauth_ui_url).
    WitFoo account Each user needs the Connect MCP permission (mcp:connect). Admin and Platform Admin have it; anyone else needs a custom role that includes it. The answers each user gets are limited to what their own role can see.
    AI app A Claude or ChatGPT plan that lets you add a custom connector or MCP server. On some business plans only a workspace administrator can add or allow one.

    Air-gapped or privacy-sensitive deployments

    The relay is off by default. An air-gapped or privacy-sensitive deployment should leave it off. Read Privacy before you turn it on.

    Make sure Conductor ingests your data sources¶

    An AI app can only answer from what WitFoo has seen. Before you connect one, make sure the Conductor is collecting from the systems you want to ask about: firewalls, endpoint tools, identity providers, cloud accounts, email security and the rest.

    • Push sources (syslog, Beats and similar) send to the Conductor's listeners: see Signal Server and the Parser Catalog.
    • Pull sources (vendor APIs) are collected by Conductor integrations: see the Integration Guides.
    • The Integration Catalog lists every parser, pull connector, exporter and processor in the current release.
    • NetFlow and IPFIX go to UDP port 2055 (published by WFA 2.5.1 and later (install WFA 2.5.5, the current release); the collector needs analytics 1.2.0 or later). The collector is off until you switch it on: in the Conductor UI, Admin → Settings → Log Servers, turn on UDP/2055 (NetFlow / IPFIX) (see Log Servers).
    • Check that data is flowing: Signals → Search shows recent artifacts, and the Conductor UI Dashboard shows message throughput through each pipeline stage.

    Why it matters for AI answers, and for tokens¶

    AI apps are billed, and limited, by the token: every word the model reads costs. A model that is handed raw logs spends most of its budget reading lines that do not matter, and still has to work out how they relate.

    WitFoo does that work before any model sees the data. The Conductor parses and shapes every signal, and Analytics builds the graph, scores suspicion, and groups related activity into work units with their evidence, reports and compliance status. What reaches the systems downstream is more than ninety percent smaller, and every record in it points back to its raw evidence. That is not sampling. Nothing security-relevant is discarded.

    So when you ask Claude or ChatGPT a question, WitFoo's tools answer with that finished record: a work unit with its suspicion score and linked evidence, a compliance status, an executive summary. The model reads a short, structured answer instead of thousands of log lines, which keeps each question small and the answer grounded in your data.

    How many tokens a question uses depends on the question, the app and the model. Ask for work units, summaries and reports first, and use artifact search for a specific follow-up rather than as a starting point.

    Turn on the relay on the appliance¶

    An administrator does this once per appliance, at the appliance's shell. See also AI-Connector Relay.

    Requires analytics 1.2.1 or later

    The relay itself needs WFA 2.5.1 or later (install WFA 2.5.5, the current release) and analytics 1.2.0 or later. The single address (https://mcp-relay.witfoo.com/mcp) needs WFA 2.5.5 and analytics 1.2.1.

    1. Turn on the MCP HTTP transport. In /witfoo/configs/node.json, set:

      {
  "mcp_http_enabled": true,
  "mcp_oauth_ui_url": "https://analytics.example.com"
}

      mcp_oauth_ui_url is the https address at which your users' browsers reach the WitFoo web UI. Then run wfa restart.

    2. Turn on the relay.

      wfa mcp-relay enable

      It shows WitFoo's relay notice. Read it, then type ENABLE (anything else leaves the relay off). The command registers the appliance and, when the appliance supports it, sets the single address as the sign-in address (mcp_public_url). It fills that field only when it is empty: if mcp_public_url already holds another address, it is left as it is and app sign-in stays at that address. Then run:

      wfa restart

    3. Note the three lines it prints (you can show them again at any time with wfa mcp-relay status):

      Address for Claude, ChatGPT and other apps that sign in: https://mcp-relay.witfoo.com/mcp
Appliance code (type it when an app asks which appliance): <your appliance code>
Address for clients that send a personal key: https://mcp-relay.witfoo.com/h/<your appliance code>/mcp

      The appliance code is 26 characters of lower-case letters and the digits 2 to 7. Give it to your users: they type it on the sign-in page. It identifies your appliance; it is not a password, and on its own it gives no access to WitFoo.

    4. If the first line names your appliance's own relay address instead, the command says why (for example, the appliance runs an analytics version older than 1.2.1). Once the requirement is met, or if you turned the relay on before you upgraded, switch with:

      wfa mcp-relay sign-in universal
wfa restart

      Apps that were connected at the old address must sign in again after a switch.

    AI Setup → MCP relay in the WitFoo web UI also shows the sign-in address and the appliance code (copyable), while the relay is on and the appliance uses the single address (it needs the mcp:manage permission).

    Allow app sign-in for your organization¶

    An administrator does this once per organization, in the WitFoo web UI. Step 1 needs mcp:manage (the AI Setup page also needs ai:manage); the client policy in steps 2 and 3 needs the admin permission. See also App Sign-in (OAuth 2.1).

    1. Open AI Setup → MCP Authentication and choose Browser chat sessions, personal keys and app sign-in, then save. If the page says App sign-in is not available on this appliance, the appliance's sign-in address or mcp_oauth_ui_url is not set to a valid value: check the previous section.
    2. Open AI Setup → MCP Authentication → Client policy. The default mode, allowlist, admits no app until you add it.
      • Claude: use Add to the allow list and choose Claude (https://claude.ai/oauth/mcp-oauth-client-metadata).
      • ChatGPT: add the host entry chatgpt.com. ChatGPT's client document address varies, and a host entry admits every document at that host.
    3. Still in Client policy, check most access. It is read-only by default, which means an app can read but never make changes, whatever a user approves. Raise it only if you want users to be able to let an app make changes. Then choose Save policy.

    Client metadata documents must be on

    The single address only works for apps that identify themselves with a published client metadata document (CIMD). This is on by default (mcp_oauth_cimd). If your appliance has it off, apps sign in as registered clients at the per-appliance address instead (Other ways to connect).

    Add WitFoo in Claude or ChatGPT¶

    Each user does this once in each app. You need the appliance code from your administrator.

    WitFoo in the app directories

    WitFoo is listed in Claude's connector directory: claude.ai/directory/witfoo. The listing uses the single connector address, so it needs analytics 1.2.1 or later and WFA 2.5.5 or later on the appliance, with the relay and app sign-in set up as above. On analytics 1.2.0, add WitFoo in Claude as a custom connector at your appliance's own address instead (see Connect at your appliance's own address).

    WitFoo is not listed in the ChatGPT app directory. In ChatGPT, add WitFoo as a custom connector, as below.

    The apps change their menu names from time to time. The steps below are as of November 2026.

    To add WitFoo from Claude's directory instead of as a custom connector, open the WitFoo listing, connect it, and continue at step 3.

    1. In Claude, open Customize → Connectors and choose Add custom connector.
    2. Name it WitFoo, enter https://mcp-relay.witfoo.com/mcp, and choose Connect.
    3. A browser window opens at connect.witfoo.com. Follow Sign in through connect.witfoo.com.
    4. Back in Claude, WitFoo shows as connected. In a chat, make sure WitFoo is switched on in the tools menu.

    You do not need developer mode.

    1. In ChatGPT on the web (chatgpt.com), open Plugins.
    2. Choose the + button, then Add custom MCP server.
    3. Name it WitFoo and enter https://mcp-relay.witfoo.com/mcp as the server URL.
    4. Choose OAuth for authentication, with a client metadata document (CIMD) rather than static credentials. Read the risk warning, choose I understand and want to continue, then Create as a plugin.
    5. When ChatGPT opens connect.witfoo.com, follow Sign in through connect.witfoo.com.
    6. Install the plugin. In a chat, type @ and choose WitFoo.

    On a ChatGPT business plan, a workspace administrator may need to allow WitFoo before members can connect it. These steps follow OpenAI's documentation as of November 2026; if your plan's menus differ, tell WitFoo support.

    Sign in through connect.witfoo.com¶

    1. Enter your appliance code. The page, Connect your WitFoo appliance, says An app at asks to connect to your WitFoo appliance. Check that the app host is the app you are connecting (for example, Claude's or ChatGPT's own domain), type your appliance code, and choose Continue. You can also paste the per-appliance address, https://mcp-relay.witfoo.com/h/<code>/mcp.
    2. Check the address on the next page. It shows your appliance's own sign-in address in large text: Your appliance's own sign-in page is at
      . Continue only if this is your organisation's WitFoo appliance, the same address you use to reach WitFoo. If it is not, do not continue: use Report this address and tell your administrator.
    3. Choose Continue to . Your browser opens your appliance's approval page.

    The approval page, on your appliance¶

    This page is part of your WitFoo web UI, titled Connect an app to WitFoo. Sign in to WitFoo if it asks (it asks when your last sign-in is more than 15 minutes old). It shows:

    • who you are signed in as, and your organization;
    • the app asking to connect: its name (declared by the app itself) and its address;
    • where the approval is delivered (the app's return address), with a warning if that is on your own computer or on a shared hosting service;
    • This app will be able to read what you can read in WitFoo;
    • an option, off by default, Also let this app make changes for you, listing the tools that make changes. It appears only when the app asked for it and your organization allows it.

    Choose Approve. Your browser returns to the app, which is now connected. You get an App connected notification in WitFoo saying what the app can do and where to revoke it.

    First questions to try¶

    Start with questions that WitFoo answers from its finished record:

    • "Using WitFoo, what are my open work units with the highest suspicion scores?"
    • "Summarize work unit : what happened, which hosts and accounts are involved, and which observations matched."
    • "Give me the latest WitFoo executive summary." (It reads the latest stored report, not a period you choose.)
    • "What is our compliance readiness for each framework?"
    • "Which of our security tools are contributing the most detections?"
    • "What does WitFoo know about the IP address
      ?"
    • "Is my WitFoo appliance healthy right now?"

    Each answer is limited to what your WitFoo role can see. If the app says a tool was refused, your role does not include that permission: ask your administrator.

    Read and write tools, and permissions¶

    From analytics 1.2.1 the MCP server has 58 tools: 35 that only read, and 23 that make changes (analytics 1.2.0 has 44, of which 28 only read).

    • Read tools search and read: work units and their evidence, graph nodes, artifacts, dashboards, reports, audit findings, chat, observations and system health.
    • Write tools make changes: update a work unit's status, add a note or a task, create a dashboard, run a playbook or a responder job, send a chat message, generate a report, and so on.

    Three things decide what an app can do, and the narrowest wins:

    1. Your WitFoo role. The app acts as you, with your permissions, never more.
    2. What you approved. An approval is read-only unless you turn on Also let this app make changes for you.
    3. Your organization's client policy. Its most access setting (read-only by default) caps what any app that signs in with a client metadata document can be given.

    Every tool states three labels for the AI apps: whether it only reads, whether it can change or remove existing data, and whether it reaches outside your appliance (running a playbook or a responder job can). An AI app can use these labels, for example to ask you before it calls a tool that makes a change.

    Limits¶

    • 60 seconds a call through the relay. The relay waits up to 60 seconds for your appliance to answer each request. A tool call that takes longer ends with an error instead of the tool's answer. Most tools answer in a few seconds; generate_report is the one most likely to come close, so ask for a smaller report or a shorter period if it times out. At your appliance's own address the limit is the reverse proxy's 120 seconds (see Connecting AI Clients & Marketplaces).
    • Request budget. Each user and app gets your organization's MCP rate limit (60 requests a minute by default), and a call to a tool that makes changes counts as four. An app that goes over it is told to wait.

    Revoke or disconnect¶

    • One app, for yourself: in WitFoo, Profile → Security → Connected apps, revoke the app. This ends its access at once. Removing the connector inside Claude or ChatGPT alone does not revoke the approval on your appliance, so revoke it in WitFoo as well.
    • Any app, for anyone in the organization (administrator): AI Setup → MCP Authentication → Connected apps in this organization.
    • An app for everyone: add it to the client policy's deny list. Saving a policy that denies an app revokes its approvals in the organization. Removing an app from the allow list only stops new approvals; to end access it already has, revoke its approvals as above.
    • All apps: choose an authentication mode without app sign-in under AI Setup → MCP Authentication. This revokes every app approval in the organization.
    • The relay for this appliance: at the appliance shell run wfa mcp-relay off, type REVOKE, then wfa restart. Revocation is permanent: turning the relay on again gives a new appliance code, and every app must be connected again.

    Troubleshooting¶

    What you see What to do
    That is not an appliance code. What you typed is not 26 letters and digits or a relay address. Copy the code from AI Setup → MCP relay, or ask your administrator (wfa mcp-relay status).
    No appliance is registered with that code, or its relay was turned off. Check the code with your administrator. A revoked relay gives a new code.
    Your appliance is not connected to the relay right now. The appliance's connection to the relay is down. The administrator runs wfa mcp-relay status and checks that the appliance can reach mcp-relay.witfoo.com on port 443.
    Your appliance did not answer. The appliance is connected but did not answer within ten seconds. Try again; check that the appliance is not overloaded.
    This appliance signs apps in at its own relay address. The appliance is not set to the single address, or is older than analytics 1.2.1. The administrator runs wfa mcp-relay sign-in universal and wfa restart, or users connect at the per-appliance address.
    Your appliance's sign-in details are not ones this page can use. Client metadata documents are off on the appliance, or its web UI address is not an https address that can be shown safely (for example, an internationalized xn-- host name). See Other ways to connect.
    A plain page reading only error code: 502 or error code: 504 An appliance that did not answer or signs apps in at its own relay address, sign-in details this page cannot use, an answer not in a form this page can use, or a tool call that ran past 60 seconds, shown by Cloudflare's own error page. Try again; if it repeats, the administrator runs wfa mcp-relay status.
    Your appliance is busy right now, Too many requests or Too many sign-ins for that appliance Wait a minute and try again.
    The address on the Continue page is not your WitFoo Do not continue. Use Report this address and tell your administrator.
    Your browser cannot open the approval page Your browser must reach the WitFoo web UI. Connect the way you normally reach WitFoo (for example, VPN) and start again from the app.
    Your administrator has not allowed this app to connect The administrator adds the app under AI Setup → MCP Authentication → Client policy.
    This appliance could not fetch the app's metadata document The appliance could not reach the app's published document. The administrator checks the appliance's outbound access, or registers the app under OAuth clients and connects at the per-appliance address.
    Your account cannot connect apps here Your role lacks the Connect MCP permission, or your account belongs to another organization.
    This appliance's license does not include connecting apps The licence is not Analytics Pro, Analytics Max or Reporter Pro.
    This app asked to make changes, but your organization allows read-only access The client policy's most access is read-only. Ask your administrator if you need changes.
    The app says a tool was refused Your WitFoo role does not hold that tool's permission, or the app was approved read-only.
    A tool call ends in an error after about a minute It ran past the relay's 60-second limit. See Limits.
    The app worked, then asks you to sign in again Your approval was revoked, your sessions were ended, the administrator switched the sign-in address, the app had not refreshed its access for 14 days, or the connection reached its 90-day limit. Sign in again from the app.
    A personal key is refused at https://mcp-relay.witfoo.com/mcp Personal keys work only at the per-appliance address, https://mcp-relay.witfoo.com/h/<code>/mcp.
    AI Setup → MCP relay shows Status unavailable Run wfa mcp-relay status. If it says contested, another party may hold the relay credential: run wfa mcp-relay off, wfa mcp-relay enable and wfa restart, and rotate your personal keys.

    Known limitations¶

    • The sign-in page at connect.witfoo.com is in English only.
    • Users of every customer who reach the relay from one AI provider's addresses share that address's relay limits.
    • Anyone with a WitFoo licence can run an appliance, so a code typed by mistake can lead to someone else's appliance. Always check the address on the Continue page.
    • If WitFoo stops the single-address sign-in, apps cannot sign in through it until it is back. An administrator can switch the appliance to its own relay address with wfa mcp-relay sign-in handle and wfa restart.

    Other ways to connect¶

    Connect at your appliance's own address¶

    Any appliance on analytics 1.2.0 or later with an Analytics Pro, Analytics Max or Reporter Pro licence and MCP turned on can connect Claude or ChatGPT at its own address, with no relay. Claude's and ChatGPT's servers make the calls, not your computer, so they must be able to reach that address from the internet: publish it through the exposure listener (port 8444) behind a proxy or load balancer with a certificate for the published name. Nothing passes through WitFoo's relay.

    1. Turn MCP on and set the appliance's public MCP address: see Turning MCP On and App Sign-in (OAuth 2.1).
    2. Allow app sign-in and add the app to the client policy, as in Allow app sign-in for your organization.
    3. Add the connector in Claude or ChatGPT as in Add WitFoo in Claude or ChatGPT, but enter your appliance's address (for example https://mcp.example.com/mcp). The app goes straight to your appliance's sign-in and approval page; there is no connect.witfoo.com step and no appliance code.

    See Connecting AI Clients & Marketplaces for desktop clients, personal keys and the other routes.

    The per-appliance relay address¶

    Every appliance with the relay on (WFA 2.5.1 or later, install WFA 2.5.5, and analytics 1.2.0 or later) also has its own relay address, https://mcp-relay.witfoo.com/h/<appliance code>/mcp. Apps can sign in there when the appliance is set to its own relay address, and it is the address for clients that send a personal MCP key (created under Profile → Security → MCP connections). A connector shared by a whole team takes one key: give it the key of a dedicated user who holds only the permissions the connector needs, never an administrator's. See AI-Connector Relay.

    Privacy¶

    Your security data stays on your appliance. It leaves only as the answer to a question an app asks on your behalf.

    • What the AI provider sees. Your questions, and the answers WitFoo's tools return, are processed by the app's provider (Anthropic for Claude, OpenAI for ChatGPT) under that provider's own terms and your plan's settings.
    • What connect.witfoo.com sees. The app's sign-in request (including its query), the appliance code you type, and the page that names your appliance's sign-in address. It never sees the username or password you enter on your appliance. The page runs no script and sets no cookie of its own.
    • What the relay keeps. Requests and answers pass through WitFoo's relay. It reads each one in memory only while it passes it on, and writes none of the content, tokens or keys to its database or logs. It writes no sign-in request, token or network address from the sign-in page either. Its logs record each request, including each request to the sign-in page: an identifier, the method, the path, the time, the status, the sizes, the duration and the outcome, and your appliance's handle once the code you typed names an appliance whose relay is on. The logs are kept for 30 days.
    • Cloudflare serves mcp-relay.witfoo.com and connect.witfoo.com and sees this traffic in transit. Its security records can include the sign-in request, and are kept for up to 31 days. If Cloudflare's protection decides to check your browser, it shows its own check page first, which runs Cloudflare's script and can set Cloudflare's cookie.
    • Off by default. The relay is off for every organization and every appliance until an administrator turns it on and acknowledges WitFoo's relay notice.
    • At your appliance's own address, nothing passes through WitFoo's relay or connect.witfoo.com.

    The full description is in the WitFoo privacy policy, section 5, "The AI-assistant connector", and in the relay's notice.
